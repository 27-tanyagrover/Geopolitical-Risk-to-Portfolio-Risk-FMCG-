# Geopolitics to Portfolio Risk: Impact on the Indian FMCG Sector

## Project Overview
This project examines how the 2025–2026 Israel–Iran conflict and the wider West Asia escalation affected the Indian FMCG sector. It studies five companies: Hindustan Unilever (HUL), ITC, Nestlé India, Britannia Industries, and Dabur India.

The objective was to understand how geopolitical events transmit into financial markets through changes in crude oil and palm oil prices, currency movements, inflation, interest rates, company profitability, and stock valuations. The project connects global events with real market performance and corporate financial data to understand their implications for investment and credit risk.

## Analysis Performed

### 1. Geopolitical Risk Assessment
- Analysed how the conflict and the threat to the Strait of Hormuz could affect crude oil prices, India's import bill, the rupee, inflation, and government bond yields.
- Studied the impact of rising crude and palm oil prices on packaging, freight, and other FMCG input costs.
- Mapped the transmission of geopolitical shocks from global commodity markets to corporate earnings and equity valuations.

### 2. Stock Market Performance Analysis
- Compared stock price returns of HUL, ITC, Nestlé India, Britannia, and Dabur over the event window from 2 March to 17 September 2026.
- Evaluated volatility, beta, Jensen's alpha, Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, Treynor ratio, and maximum drawdown.
- Compared individual company performance with the Nifty FMCG Index to identify relative winners, neutral performers, and laggards.

### 3. Financial Statement and Credit Risk Analysis
- Examined quarterly revenue and profit performance, particularly changes in profit after tax (PAT) margins before and after the escalation.
- Analysed debt-to-equity ratios and interest coverage ratios to compare the financial strength of the five companies.
- Evaluated how leverage, interest coverage, and cost pressures influence a company's ability to absorb prolonged economic shocks.

### 4. Stress Testing and Scenario Analysis
- Developed a sensitivity analysis using a hypothetical scenario of a 20% increase in crude oil prices, an 8–10% increase in palm oil prices, and 2% rupee depreciation against the US dollar.
- Assessed the potential impact on EBITDA margins, PAT margins, operating cash flow, and stock valuations.
- Compared the relative vulnerability of the five companies under the assumed stress scenario.

### 5. Portfolio Risk Management
- Evaluated whether holding FMCG stocks alone provides adequate protection during geopolitical crises.
- Explored portfolio rebalancing, gold exposure, commodity hedging, currency hedging, bond duration management, and protective index put options.
- Developed an illustrative ₹100-crore portfolio allocation overlay to demonstrate how risk exposure could be adjusted during a market shock.

## Key Findings
- The Nifty FMCG Index declined approximately 10.2% during the selected event window, challenging the assumption that consumer staples always act as defensive investments.
- Nestlé India was the only company among the five to generate a positive stock return, while Dabur India experienced the steepest decline.
- HUL and ITC experienced significant PAT margin compression in Q1 FY27, highlighting the delayed impact of cost pressures on reported earnings.
- Britannia had the highest debt-to-equity ratio among the five companies, while ITC had the strongest interest coverage.
- The analysis highlighted that geopolitical shocks can affect companies differently depending on their input costs, pricing power, business exposure, leverage, and ability to pass higher costs on to consumers.

## Key Learnings
Through this project, I learned how to connect geopolitical developments with financial market movements and corporate financial performance rather than analysing them as separate events.

I developed a better understanding of how crude oil price shocks, palm oil costs, currency depreciation, and rising bond yields can affect company margins, stock valuations, and investor returns. I also learned how to use risk-adjusted performance measures to compare companies beyond their absolute stock returns and how financial ratios help assess their ability to withstand economic uncertainty.

Another important learning was the practical application of scenario analysis and stress testing to estimate the potential financial impact of adverse market conditions. The project also helped me understand portfolio diversification, hedging strategies, and the importance of balancing investment returns against downside risk.

Overall, this project strengthened my skills in financial analysis, risk assessment, data interpretation, and evidence-based investment decision-making.

## Tools and Concepts Used
- **Data:** Daily stock price and return series, quarterly financial results, and company solvency data.
- **Analysis:** Stock return analysis, volatility, beta, Jensen's alpha, Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, Treynor ratio, and maximum drawdown.
- **Financial concepts:** Credit risk, commodity risk, currency risk, interest-rate risk, scenario analysis, stress testing, portfolio allocation, and hedging strategies
